## 1. Visitor jobs and the museum's job

### 1.1 Who arrives, and from where

Visitors do not arrive knowing a bucket exists. Realistic entry paths: a shared media link whose domain they then explore; a link from hues.flowers (Music page, Dahlias page) into "the collection"; search; plain curiosity about the farm's pictures and music. Almost all first visits are on a phone, outdoors-grade light and one thumb are normal conditions, and most visitors have no task more specific than *"show me what's here."* The design therefore leads with browsing and recognition, and treats search as the accelerator for the minority who arrive with a name in mind — the reverse of a directory tool.

> **Audience annotation (gate F4):** the framing in this section — and wherever this document says "visitor" or "first-time visitor" — was drafted against public strangers. The locked audience (GOAL.md, Dustin's final word) is **Dustin + the team**; public access is a build simplification only, with no promotion and no indexing. Read every "visitor" below as a team member who knows the park, arriving at a familiar collection. The jobs and design are unchanged — browsing-first still serves that audience — but no sentence in this document should be read as designing for, or marketing to, the general public.

### 1.2 Visitor jobs, ranked

| Rank | Job | What success looks like | Primary surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Wander the collection** — browse beautiful things with no specific target | Within one tap of landing, the visitor is looking at pictures or hearing music, and always knows what else there is to see | Entrance (§3.1), wing galleries (§3.2) |
| J2 | **Find one named thing** — a dahlia variety they grew / saw at the park, a song they heard there | Type or tap the name, land on that piece, without learning any structure | Search (§3.6), Dahlias A–Z index (§3.3) |
| J3 | **Look closely / listen properly** — see a photo large, hear a whole track | One tap from any card to a full piece view; music keeps playing while they keep wandering | Piece view (§4), Listening Room (§3.4) |
| J4 | **Take it with them** — share a piece, copy a link, see the original file | Share/copy actions on every piece view; the piece's page link and its direct media link are two clearly labelled, different things | Piece view actions (§4.2) |
| J5 | **Go on to Hues** — visit the park, hear more, see the main site | Every route carries a calm exit to hues.flowers; the museum is a room of the park, not a cul-de-sac | Footer + About strip (§3.1) |
| J6 (team, secondary) | **Find an asset to reuse** — Hue/team locating a known file's page | Served by the same search + copy-media-link actions; explicitly *not* a design driver, and no team-only tooling is added to a public surface | Search, piece view |

### 1.3 The museum's job, in one line

**Turn an invisible file store into a place: five galleries a phone visitor can wander, search, and share from — without ever changing what a media URL does.** (Audience per the F4 annotation at §1.1: in practice that visitor is Dustin or a team member.)

### 2.3 The routing contract — how SPA deep links and media URLs coexist

This is the contract the build must implement in `hues-media-server` and Quinn must gate. Order matters; rules are evaluated top-down.

- **R-1 · Media wins first, always.** If the path resolves to an existing R2 key (after the existing `cdn-cgi/image/` normalisation), serve the file exactly as today — range, immutable cache, CORS, transform fallback untouched. No app route pattern is ever consulted before this check.
- **R-2 · Extensions are files.** Any path whose last segment contains a file extension (`.jpg`, `.mp3`, `.webp`, …) that misses R-1 is a **true media 404** (plain, as today) — never the SPA. A browser asking for bytes must never receive HTML; that failure is silent and corrupts players and image tags downstream.
- **R-3 · Known media prefixes are files.** Any multi-segment path under `hues/`, `music/`, `seeds/` (future), `_app/`, or `cdn-cgi/` that misses R-1 is likewise a true media 404.
- **R-4 · App routes fall back to the SPA.** Only these shapes reach the app shell: `/`, the five wing roots, `/search`, and two-segment piece/track paths under the five wing slugs whose second segment is extensionless and not a reserved word (R-5). The Worker serves `_app/index.html` with a **short cache** (never the immutable media cache) and status 200.
- **R-5 · Reserved second segments.** Under `/music`, the segment `tracks` is reserved to the media namespace forever. The catalog must never mint a track slug of `tracks` (nor any slug containing a `.`). Equivalent guard for future wings: a piece slug may not equal any media sub-prefix name in its wing's source tree.
- **R-6 · The `/seeds` reservation.** `/seeds` (one segment) is reserved as a future app route; `/seeds/<anything>` is media namespace from the day the prefix lands. Until the wing opens, `/seeds` returns the museum 404 like any unbuilt route (served under R-9: shell at 200 rendering the 404 view) — no "coming soon" placeholder, which would promise what the cutover schedule hasn't.
- **R-7 · Piece URLs ≠ media URLs, by design.** A piece page (`/dahlias/labyrinth`) and its file (`/hues/cultivar-photos/…`) are different URLs with different jobs: the page is for people, the file is for machines and hotlinking. Every piece view exposes both, labelled (§4.2), and neither redirects to the other.
- **R-8 · Method and HEAD behaviour unchanged** for media. The SPA fallback applies to `GET` only; the existing 405 for other methods on media paths stands.
- **R-9 · Terminal rule — no path is undefined (gate F13).** Any path not claimed by R-1–R-8 lands here: extensionless, outside every media prefix, and not an R-4 app shape — a mistyped wing name (`/dahila`), an unknown multi-segment path (`/foo/bar`), any stale or invented app-shaped URL. It is served the app shell exactly as R-4 serves it (status 200, short cache), and the app renders the museum 404 view (§3.7). It is never a plain-text media 404 — R-2/R-3 have already claimed everything that could be a file — and never a bare Worker 404. With R-9, every possible path has exactly one specified outcome, which is the contract's whole purpose.

**Deep-link behaviour that follows:** a visitor opening `/dahlias/labyrinth` cold gets the app shell, the catalog loads, the piece renders with a correct back path to `/dahlias` (never a stranded detail page). A visitor opening a media URL cold gets the file, full stop — the museum never interposes itself between a live site and its images.

### 2.4 State in the URL (house dynamic-state discipline)

Local-scope state a visitor might share, bookmark, or return to lives in the URL, updated via the History API with no document load: gallery search text (`?q=`), Dahlias letter (`?letter=`), sort (`?sort=` — **reserved, unused in v1**: no sort control exists, §3.3; the parameter is named here only so a future sort adopts it deliberately rather than inventing a second one), Listening Room voice filter (`?voice=`), global search (`/search?q=`). One deliberate change = exactly one history entry; re-tapping the active option = no entry, no change; Back/Forward restore the prior state **in place**, including result count and scroll anchor. None of these changes fire a page-view count. Playback state (current track, position, queue) is the stated exception: it lives in the app shell, not the URL — a shared link should start the song, not inherit someone's mid-song position.

## 3. Screen-by-screen UX

Conventions for every screen below: phone-first layout described first, desktop differences called out explicitly; all touch targets ≥ 44 px; every list control follows the in-place standard (acknowledge ≤ 0.1 s, results paint in the INP good band, prior content stays visible during any fetch, no spinner or skeleton for a sub-second local update).

### 3.0 App shell (every screen)

- **Purpose:** orientation that never changes under the visitor — where am I, what else is here, how do I get to Hues.
- **Blocks, in order:** (1) Header — museum wordmark (links to `/`), wing navigation, search action. (2) Screen content. (3) Persistent music player bar — rendered **only while a track is loaded** (§3.4); when absent it occupies no space. (4) Footer — one-line about, link to hues.flowers, "media served from this address" colophon line (structural label; Wren writes final copy).
- **Navigation behaviour:** wing nav is **visible and labelled on every screen** — never icon-only, never hover-revealed. Desktop: all five wings in the header. Phone: header carries the wordmark + search; the five wings sit in a labelled strip directly under the header that **wraps to three rows at 320–359 px** rather than scrolling sideways (reflow rule, §5.3) — full wing names are kept at every width (Dustin's ruling, 2026-11-26); the three-row wrap at the narrowest phones is the decided layout, superseding the two-row expectation Mina flagged as FLAG-N1 — current wing marked with `aria-current="page"` plus a non-colour distinction (weight + underline/rule), never colour alone. A "Galleries" disclosure is the fallback only if Mina's layout cannot hold the wrapped strip; hidden primary navigation is a documented discoverability loss and needs Hue's sign-off, not a silent default.
- **Sticky behaviour:** the header may stick; the player bar docks at the **bottom** on phone (thumb reach) and must never obscure focused content — content gets bottom padding equal to player height while it is present, and focus scroll-margin keeps the focused element clear of both bars.
- **States:** catalog not yet loaded → shell renders immediately with the content region in skeleton (§3.8); catalog failed → shell stays, content region shows the error state. The shell never blanks.

### 3.1 Entrance — `/`

- **One job:** get a first-time visitor looking at something beautiful within one tap, and teach the five-wing map by showing it.
- **Blocks, in order:**
  1. **Title block** — museum name as the page's single display moment, one-line plain-language statement of what this place is (structural placeholder; Wren writes it), and the collection's scale stated honestly ("218 dahlias · 19 songs" — counts come from the catalog, never hard-coded; the 38 recordings are counted in the Listening Room, not here).
  2. **Search field** — prominent, labelled for what it searches ("Search dahlias, artwork and songs"), submits to `/search?q=`. This is the J2 accelerator; it sits above the wing directory because a visitor with a name in mind should not have to walk past five doors first.
  3. **Wing directory** — five entry cards in job order: **Dahlias, The Park, Listening Room, Art & Motifs, Arcade.** Each card: one representative image (catalog `featured` piece for that wing), wing name, exhibit count, one-line scent of what's inside. Whole card is one link, one tap target.
  4. **Highlights** — a curated cross-wing selection (8–12 pieces, catalog `featured` flag + `highlightOrder`), mixed images and at least two songs, each labelled with its wing. This is the Peak-End / Von Restorff moment of the entrance: the one place the museum says "start here." Songs in highlights play in place via the shell player; tapping a highlight image goes to its piece view.
  5. **About strip** — two or three sentences: this is Hues Floral Park's media collection; the same files the websites use, hung where people can wander them. Exit action: **Visit hues.flowers**.
- **Phone vs desktop:** phone = single column, wing cards full-width with image above label; desktop = wing directory becomes a true gallery entrance — first card (Dahlias) spans double width, the rest in a 2–3 column arrangement, highlights in a multi-column grid. Desktop is a composed page, not a stretched phone column (house rule: two real designs under one URL).
- **States:** loading → skeleton matching the real block geometry (title block, card shapes, highlight grid); error (catalog failed) → §3.8 error state with retry, plus a plain statement that the media files themselves are unaffected and a link to hues.flowers — honesty over theatre; empty → impossible by construction (catalog ships with exhibits), but if the catalog returns zero exhibits the entrance degrades to title + About + error-style notice, never a blank page.

### 3.2 Wing gallery template — image wings (`/park`, `/art`, and the base of `/dahlias`, `/arcade`)

- **One job:** let the visitor see everything in this wing, grouped the way they'd group it, and reach any piece in one tap.
- **Blocks, in order:**
  1. **Wing head** — wing name (H1), exhibit count as a persistent live region (`aria-live="polite"`, present before any change), one-line intro (Wren), and — where the catalog supplies groups — group jump links (e.g. Park: Beds & Blooms / The Grounds / Seasons / People).
  2. **Controls row** (only where the wing's scale earns it — Park may ship with none beyond group jumps; see per-wing notes): search-within-wing field, sort control, filter chips. Every control is local-scope (§2.4).
  3. **Applied-state chips** — any active search/filter appears as removable chips directly above the results, with a Clear all action. Never hidden inside a closed panel.
  4. **Results** — image grid; each cell is one exhibit: image, title below the image on the paper ground (**not** text over the photo — §5.3), and a set/group label where applicable. Whole cell is the link to the piece view.
  5. **End matter** — when the full wing is shown: a quiet end line with the wing's exhibit count **from the catalog** ("That's the whole wing — {count} pieces"; the count is data, never a prefix count — an earlier draft's "85 pieces" example conflated the `m-art/` *prefix* count with a *wing* count and is struck, gate F19) and a next-wing link (Serial Position: the exit at the end is the one they remember).
- **Long-list mechanics (all image wings):** the grid renders an initial **48 exhibits**, then a **Show more** button appends the next 48 **in place** — scroll position, focus (the button keeps focus, its label updates to the new showing count), and surrounding content do not move. No numbered pagination: a museum wall is continuous, and pagination would break the return-to-place behaviour (§4.4). Images below the fold lazy-load; every image reserves its aspect-ratio box before load (CLS ≤ 0.1); thumbnails request the existing transform ladder (320/480 px widths) — the original file is never the thumbnail.
- **Phone vs desktop:** phone grid 2 columns; desktop 3–4 columns with larger cells and group headings spanning the row. Controls row sticks under the header on both while scrolling a long wing (with the applied chips), because losing the filter controls mid-list is a documented Baymard failure.
- **States:** first load → grid-shaped skeleton; local update (search/filter/sort/show-more) → **no skeleton, no spinner** — current grid stays visible, count updates in step with results; zero results → §3.8 zero state naming the active filter and offering its removal (one tap per chip, plus Clear all); catalog error → error region in place of the grid, wing head and nav intact, retry action.

### 3.3 Dahlias — `/dahlias` (the scale problem, solved)

218 near-identical-format exhibits is the museum's one real scale problem. A flat scroll of 218 fails Hick's Law and choice-overload research; a search-only design fails the wander job (J1 outranks J2). The design layers three paths over one list, all in-place:

> **Count & provenance annotation (gate F3/F14):** this section was drafted against "412 exhibits," a file count. The verified exhibit count is **218 varieties** (§2.1), and the 206 pool photographs are game-pool imagery of the varieties, not park-camera photographs — only the round-2 set is park-photographed. Figures in this section are corrected to 218; the design is untouched, because the machinery was always sized by the list, not by the integer.

1. **Highlights first.** The wing opens with a curated **Featured dahlias** band (12–16 pieces, catalog `featured`) — a recommended starting point before the full index, so a wanderer meets the best wall first and a seeker scrolls past it in one gesture.
2. **A–Z letter index.** A sticky letter bar (A–Z, letters with no varieties disabled and visibly so, each letter a ≥ 44 px target) jumps within the page to letter groups; the full list is grouped under letter headings. Selecting a letter sets `?letter=` and filters in place; **All** clears it. Counts ride along: the bar's accessible name and the results count announce "Dahlias beginning with L — 23" via the persistent live region.
3. **Search-within-wing.** One field filtering by variety name (and catalog keywords) as a committed search (submit or debounce-with-live-count, one history entry per committed change — never one per keystroke).

- **Sort:** default is A–Z (the index is the mental model). No other sort is offered in v1 — there is no honest second ordering in the data (no dates, no popularity), and an unearned control is clutter. Revisit if the catalog gains a field worth sorting by.
- **Filters beyond letter/search:** none in v1, deliberately. Baymard's rule — users expect a filter for every key attribute shown on the card — cuts the other way here: cards show name only, so no attribute filters are promised. If the catalog pass can supply a trustworthy **colour group** or **form** field for a large majority of the 218, that becomes a v1.1 filter-chips group (multi-select, counts beside options, OR within the group) without structural change. It is *not* designed as a launch dependency, because inventing attributes the source data may not support is how catalog passes stall.
- **Rendering:** same 48-at-a-time incremental reveal as §3.2, counted across the *filtered* set ("Showing 48 of 218" / "Showing 23 of 23 beginning with L"). Letter groups only render for the revealed portion; jumping to a letter loads that letter's full group (max group size is bounded by the alphabet — no letter can hold the whole 218).
- **Return behaviour:** leaving for a piece and coming Back restores letter, search, revealed count, and scroll position exactly (§4.4). Losing a seeker's place in a 218-list is the single worst failure this wing can commit.

### 3.4 Listening Room — `/music`

- **One job:** let a visitor hear the park's music — whole songs, in place, without leaving the museum to do it.
- **Blocks, in order:**
  1. **Room head** — "Listening Room" (H1), track/song count, one-line intro (Wren).
  2. **Voice toggle** — a three-option segmented control: **All songs / Sung / Instrumental.** Local-scope: updates the list in place, sets `?voice=`, one history entry, count announces through the live region. The distinction is carried by a text label on every row as well — never by colour or icon alone.
  3. **Track list** — one row per **recording** (38 rows), grouped under their song (reuses @hues/ui `TrackRow` pattern): play/pause button (≥ 44 px), title, voice label ("Sung" / "Instrumental") and duration (tabular figures, from the catalog — §4.3) — voice and duration are **per-recording**, since each song's two versions differ in both. The exhibit is the song (19 exhibits, §6 Q2 as decided); the list is recordings, so every version is directly playable without opening the track view. The track view carries the version switch (§4.4). The currently playing row is marked by label + icon + position, not colour alone; `poppy` is quarantined to player graphics in the token set and stays that way (§5.1).
  4. **Persistent player** — when a track is loaded, the shell player (§3.0) docks: play/pause, previous/next, progress bar with elapsed/total as text, track title + voice label, and a link to the track view. It survives client-side navigation to any other wing — hearing a song while wandering the Dahlias is the museum's best trick and a genuine SPA affordance. It never autoplays on arrival, ever.
- **Queue behaviour (specified, deliberately small):** pressing play on any row sets the queue to **the currently visible list in its current order** (respecting the voice filter), starting at that track. Next/previous walk that queue; at the end of a track the next plays (continuous listening is the room's job); at the end of the queue, playback stops and the player shows the last track paused at 0:00 — no loop, no surprise jump to another wing's media. Changing the voice filter mid-playback does **not** interrupt the playing track; the new queue applies from the next track change, and the player says so only if the visitor presses next into an empty remainder (then: stop, announce "End of the list"). Playback uses the file's existing range support for seeking; the progress bar is a real slider with an accessible name and text readout.
- **Phone vs desktop:** phone = rows full-width, player docked bottom; desktop = the list gains a second column of context (a short "about this music" block and links to featured songs sit beside the list rather than above it) — the list itself stays one column, because track rows scan vertically.
- **States:** list loading → row-shaped skeleton; a track that fails to load → the row and player show a plain-language error with a retry and a direct link to the file, and the queue advances no further on its own; filter with zero results is impossible (19/19 split guarantees both voices non-empty) but the state is designed anyway per template.

### 3.5 Arcade — `/arcade`

Same template as §3.2 with grouping as the primary structure: **one section per game** (game name as group heading, its art as the grid beneath), because 66 pieces make sense to a visitor as "the art of the games," not as a stream. Each group heading carries a text link out to that game on hues.flowers/arcade (structural action; the museum shows the art, the park hosts the play). Pieces whose game the catalog cannot name go in a final "More from the Arcade" group — never dropped silently, never invented a game for.

**One named non-game group lives in this wing (assignment, gate F22 — Quinn's recommendation adopted):** the season-blooms set (12 pieces) hangs as a named group, **Season Blooms**, after the game groups and before "More from the Arcade." The files live in the arcade/art family and Elena grouped them there; they are finished seasonal art, not a game, and the group carries its own honest name rather than an invented game. In catalog terms it is a `group` value like any other (§4.3) — the catalog pass executes it without further decision.

### 3.6 Search — `/search`

- **Job:** J2, cross-wing. One field (pre-filled from `?q=`, editable in place — editing and resubmitting updates results in place under §2.4 discipline), results **grouped by wing** with per-wing counts, each group showing up to 12 matches with a "See all N in Dahlias" action that lands on the wing with `?q=` applied. Matching is over catalog `title`, `keywords`, and wing name; songs match by title and appear with a play button inline (searching a song and having to visit its page to hear it would fail the job).
- **Zero results:** names the query, suggests checking spelling, and offers the highlights rail and wing directory as the recovery — a dead-end zero state is a designed failure, not an outcome.
- **Phone/desktop:** same structure; desktop groups may run two columns of result cards, phone stacks groups.

### 3.7 Museum 404 — app-shaped unknown paths

For a person, in the museum's voice (Wren writes the line; structure here): a plain statement that this room doesn't exist, the five wing links, a search field, and the exit to hues.flowers. **Delivery, stated honestly (gate F13): the app shell served with status 200, rendering this 404 view.** Under shell-only delivery the Worker cannot know whether a client-rendered slug exists, so an HTTP 404 status for unknown app slugs is undeliverable and is **not promised anywhere in this design** — the museum 404 is a rendered view, not a response status. Genuine HTTP 404s exist only where R-2/R-3 put them: missing media files, as plain text. This page must be **unmistakably different** from the Worker's plain-text media 404 — if Quinn's gate ever sees HTML at a missing-file URL or plain text at a missing-page URL, the routing contract (§2.3) has failed.

### 3.8 Shared states (all screens)

| State | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Loading (first load)** | Skeleton whose geometry matches the real layout exactly (wing cards, grid cells with aspect boxes, track rows). Used only for genuine full-screen loads in the ~1–10 s band. |
| **Loading (local update)** | None visible. Prior content stays; sub-second updates get no indicator. |
| **Error (catalog/data)** | In-place error region (house `ErrorRegion` pattern): plain language, what happened, Retry action, and the honest note that media files at their own URLs are unaffected. Never a blank screen, never an error code. |
| **Error (single image)** | The cell/view keeps its aspect box with a labelled broken-state and a "Open the original file" link — one bad file never takes down a wall. |
| **Empty wing** | If a wing's catalog ships empty: wing head + a short "This room is still being hung" style structural placeholder + links to other wings. (Expected only if curation defers a wing; not a launch plan.) |
| **Offline** | **No offline design, deliberately:** no PWA means no service worker and no cached shell. A failed fetch shows the standard error state. Nothing in the UI may imply the museum works offline. |
| **Long list end** | Explicit end line with total count + next-wing link (§3.2). An endless scroll with no end is not used anywhere. |

## 4. The single-piece experience

### 4.1 Piece view — images (`/:wing/:slug`)

- **One job:** let one piece be looked at properly, with its name and story attached, and make the next piece one tap away.
- **Blocks, in order:**
  1. **Back link** — labelled with the wing ("Back to Dahlias"), top and repeated at the foot. Browser Back and this link land in the same place with the same restored state.
  2. **The piece, large** — the image at the largest transform width the layout uses (up to 1920 on desktop, viewport width on phone), aspect box reserved, on the viewing ground Mina designs (§5.1 flags the deep-ground question for this surface specifically). The image itself is **not** a link: the explicit labelled "View full size" action below is the only path to the original file. (Reconciliation, gate F24: an image-as-link was specified here in the first draft and is withdrawn — it is undiscoverable as an affordance, and Mina's piece view is labelled-action-only; her treatment stands.)
  3. **Title + caption block, below the image on paper** — title (H1), wing label, short caption; where the catalog supplies a longer note, it follows in editorial type (§5.2). Never overlaid on the photo.
  4. **Details** — the honest metadata the catalog actually has: subject/variety, where this piece appears at Hues (if known), file type. Nothing is displayed that the catalog doesn't supply — no invented dates, no invented credits.
  5. **Actions row** (§4.2).
  6. **Previous / Next** — large labelled controls ("Previous dahlia", "Next dahlia" — names, not bare arrows; arrows may accompany the words). They walk the **list the visitor came from**: the wing's current filtered/searched order if they arrived from one, else the wing's default order. At the ends, the control names the exit ("Back to all Dahlias") rather than disabling silently.
  7. **More from this wing** — a short rail (6 pieces: neighbours in the current order first, then featured) so the piece view feeds the wander instead of ending it.
- **Phone vs desktop:** phone = single column, actions and prev/next in the lower thumb zone after the caption; desktop = image left (about two-thirds width), title/caption/details/actions in a right rail that sticks while the image scrolls if the image is tall; prev/next span the foot of both columns.

### 4.2 Actions on every piece (image and track)

| Action | Behaviour | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| **Copy page link** | Copies the museum URL (`/dahlias/labyrinth`); confirms in place ("Link copied") via the live region + visible label change — no toast that steals focus | J4 sharing: the page is the shareable, captioned thing |
| **Copy media link** | Copies the direct file URL; labelled distinctly from page link | J4/J6 reuse: the file is what other pages embed |
| **View full size** (images) / **Open the file** (tracks) | Opens the original media URL (R-7: a different URL, and the label says so) | The museum never walls off the original |
| **Share** (where `navigator.share` exists) | System share sheet with the page URL and title | Phone-first visitors share through the sheet they know |

No download button in v1: the original is one tap away via View full size, and a labelled Download invites bulk scraping of full-resolution files without serving any ranked visitor job. (Raised for Dustin as part of Open Question 3's context, not as a blocker.)

### 4.3 The catalog — fields every exhibit must supply

The catalog is a generated data file (the catalog/data seat builds it; Wren writes the words that need writing). It ships separately from the app bundle, under a reserved prefix with a **short cache**, so exhibits can be added without redeploying the SPA. One record per exhibit:

| Field | Required | Source / derivation | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `slug` | ✅ | Minted at catalog pass; stable forever once published; extensionless; not a reserved word (R-5) | Routes, prev/next, search |
| `title` | ✅ | Dahlias: variety name from the site's existing dahlia data. Tracks: song title from the Music page record. Art/park/arcade: named in the catalog pass (Wren/Elena) — **never the raw filename** | Cards, piece H1, search |
| `wing` | ✅ | One of the five (+ future `seeds`) | IA, grouping, back links |
| `mediaKey` | ✅ | The exact R2 key the Worker already serves | Every image/audio request |
| `mediaType` | ✅ | `image` \| `audio` (+ dimensions/format for images from file metadata) | Templates, aspect boxes |
| `altText` | ✅ (images) | Written in the catalog pass; describes the piece, transcribes any essential lettering inside artwork | §5.3 floor; screen readers |
| `captionShort` | ✅ (tracks: pattern-supplied — see the track scope note below) | One line, Wren | Cards where shown, search results, piece view |
| `captionLong` | Optional | 1–3 sentences, Wren; only where a real story exists — absence is fine, invention is not | Piece view editorial block |
| `keywords` | ✅ | Subject words visitors would type (variety synonyms, game name, subjects in a park photo) | Search |
| `group` | Where applicable | Park subject group; arcade game name (or `Season Blooms` — the Arcade wing's one named non-game group, §3.5); art set/series name | Wing grouping (§3.2/§3.5) |
| `cropFocus` | Optional (default: centre) | Per-piece focal point for card crops (e.g. `50% 30%`), set by the catalog pass from Elena's art-direction crop directions for the piece; centre when unset | Card and grid crops — Mina's `card` slot anchors on this field (her component-anchor spec is hers, in her later pass) |
| `featured` + `highlightOrder` | Optional | Curation decision in the catalog pass | Entrance highlights, Dahlias featured band |
| `whereUsed` | Optional | Where the piece appears at Hues / on the sites, when known | Piece details, team reuse (J6) |
| `exhibitStatus` | ✅ | `in` \| `out`, with internal reason when out | Enforces "curated, not a listing" |
| **Dahlia extras:** `varietyName` (= title), `colourGroup`, `form` | Optional, phase-2 | Only if the source data supports them for most of the 218 | Future filters (§3.3) |
| **Track extras:** `voice` (`vocal`\|`instrumental`) | ✅ | From the Music page record (19/19) | Toggle, row labels |
| **Track extras:** `durationSec` | ✅ | Read from file metadata at catalog build — never typed by hand | Rows, player (tabular figures) |
| **Track extras:** `companionSlug` | Optional | The other version of the same song, where a pair exists | Track view (Open Question 2) |

**Track scope (F12 ruling — mine, recorded):** for **track** exhibits, `captionShort` is **not** a required authored field. A track exhibit's minimum is `title` + `voice` + `durationSec` (+ `companionSlug` where a pair exists); its `captionShort` is supplied by Wren's track pattern (on her §6 long-tail model — a framing line generated from the version/companion data), written in her later pass, not by a per-song authored line. This keeps the catalog pass unblocked by the 17 songs with no documented story: a track with no authored caption still exhibits, wearing the pattern line; a track missing title, voice, or duration still fails the hard rule below.

**Hard rule for the catalog pass:** a missing optional field hides its UI element; a missing required field keeps the piece out of the build. The museum never renders a placeholder title, a guessed caption, or a filename wearing a title's clothes.

### 4.4 Track view — `/music/:slug`

Piece template with the listening job in front: title (H1), voice label, a large play/pause control (≥ 44 px, labelled with the song name), the shared shell player takes over playback (the track view does not run a second, competing player), companion-version link where `companionSlug` exists ("Hear the instrumental version" — wording is Wren's, structure is here), actions per §4.2, Previous/Next **track** walking **the visitor's current queue** — the list they arrived from, per §3.4's queue behaviour and §4.1's origin-list principle — falling back to the Listening Room's default order when the track view is arrived at directly (gate F25), and "More from the Listening Room" as a short track list with inline play buttons.

### 4.5 Return behaviour (applies everywhere)

Back from any piece view restores the originating gallery **exactly**: filter/search/letter state, revealed count, scroll position, and focus on the card that was opened. This is Baymard's list-return rule and the house history discipline (§2.4) applied as one requirement, and it is a Quinn gate item, not a nicety.

## 5. Foundations recommendations (for Mina, Iris, Thea's seats, and Ada)

### 5.1 Color — feeling direction (Iris's playbook applied; Ada owns every contrast ruling)

**Feeling target, in Iris's fixed order — value and chroma before hue:** built to read as *unhurried, warm, quietly joyful — a little world apart.* Evidence grade for that read: **moderate** (association literature is strong and cross-cultural for light values + gentle saturation pairing with positive, low-arousal feeling; induced-feeling effects are weak and are not claimed). The known trade is stated per Iris's standing note: this house deliberately gives up the peak pleasantness/arousal of loud, saturated palettes for quiet wonder. That trade is correct here — a museum of one's own media should feel like wandering the park at golden hour, not like a sale.

- **Value key:** light. Page ground `paper-1` #F7F1DE; raised surfaces `paper-0` #FFFDF4; recessed wells `paper-2` #F1E8CF (gold-ink text forbidden there — existing Ada ruling in the tokens). Headings/footer in the deep pine scale: `deep` #23362B, body `ink` #2E4438 (9.30:1 on paper-1), muted `pine-2` #43534A (7.22:1), faint `sage-ink` #4E5D47 (6.24:1 — the only text-weight sage).
- **Chroma budget:** neutrals + gold own the space in roughly 60-30-10 proportion. The bloom accents already in the token set (`dahlia-coral`, `dahlia-pink`, `dusty-rose`, `marigold`, `lavender`, `sky`) are reserved for **wing markers, plates, and celebration graphics only** — never text, never the only carrier of a distinction. Recommended structural use: each wing gets a marker built from **colour + label + position** together, so wing identity survives every colour-vision deficiency (Iris: encode redundantly; separate categories by lightness as well as hue). Which accent belongs to which wing is Mina/Iris's visual call; that no wing is identified by colour alone is this document's requirement.
- **Gold discipline (existing token law, restated because a museum will reach for gold constantly):** `gold-2` is graphics/rules/stitch only, never text; `gold-ink` #86691F is the only text gold (4.59:1 on paper-1, 5.08:1 on paper-0, banned on paper-2); `gold-3` serves large text and the light-ground focus ring; on deep grounds, `gold-1` #E8CF8F (8.42:1) is the accent text.
- **The Listening Room** may take the token set's evening register — `deep-2` #1C2A21 ground for the player bar (and optionally the room head) with `gold-1` accents — because a listening surface at lower value makes the paper-bright galleries feel brighter by contrast (simultaneous contrast, used deliberately) and matches how the music is actually heard at the park: evening, string lights. Text pairs on that ground must be verified pair-by-pair by Ada; this document asserts no ratio it hasn't taken from the tokens.
- **`poppy` stays quarantined** to music-player graphics, never text, and play state never rides on it alone (icon + label carry it) — existing token rule, applied here to the toggle and player.
- **House rule check:** dahlia paintings are never placed on dark green grounds — that rule governs *artwork placement* and binds Elena/Mina if painted pieces are used as wing headers or highlights art. Photographs displayed on a deep viewing ground (§5.1 flag below) are a different case, but I flag it rather than assume the distinction: **FLAG for Iris + Elena** — confirm the deep-ground piece view doesn't collide with the spirit of that rule before Mina builds it.
- **Museum-specific need, flagged not invented:** the single-piece view wants a *viewing ground* — a deep, quiet surround that lets a photograph's own colours read at full value. The token set has candidates (`deep`, `deep-2`, `frame`) but all are assigned to other jobs (headings/footer, evening, dashboard frame). **No new token is proposed here.** Mina + Iris choose among existing grounds or propose one new token through the design-system process; Ada verifies caption/UI pairs on whichever ground is chosen.

Three faces, already loaded by `@hues/fonts`, no additions: **Fraunces** (display, weight 560 instance), **Newsreader** (editorial), **Source Sans 3** (UI). Base 16 px, ratio 1.25. Roles below are the existing `sys.type` tokens — Mina uses these names, not raw sizes.

| `h2` | Fraunces · 31 px · 1.2 | Entrance section heads (Wing directory, Highlights), group headings in wings |
| `title` | Fraunces · 20 px · 1.35 | Card titles where a card carries a title line (wing entry cards, highlight cards) |
| `lede` | Newsreader · 18 px · 1.6 | Wing intros, About strip — the museum's editorial voice |

| `body` | Source Sans 3 · 16 px · 1.5 | Default UI text, details, controls' labels |

Display type is for titles only: Fraunces never sets a caption, a control, or a metadata line.

### 5.3 Accessibility constraints — Ada's floor, applied as constraints

These are requirements on Mina's design and the build, stated from the WCAG 2.2 AA floor. Where a number is needed that the token set doesn't already carry, it is **flagged for Ada's ruling** — this document does not invent rulings.

1. **Contrast:** text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, essential UI/graphics ≥ 3:1 — measured on real pairs at real sizes. The token pairs cited in §5.1 already carry measured ratios; any new pair (especially anything on the piece-view viewing ground, and anything on the evening player ground) goes to Ada before build.
2. **No essential text over photographs.** Captions and titles sit on paper below the image. If Mina proposes overlay titles on entrance/highlight cards, that requires a scrim + worst-point measurement + Ada's ruling; my recommendation is don't — the below-image pattern is already the house card pattern.
3. **Focus:** visible on every interactive element, using the token focus system (ground-following: `gold-3` ring + `deep` offset on light grounds; `gold-2` on deep grounds), never obscured by the sticky header or the docked player (scroll-margin is part of the build spec), logical order through grid → controls → player.
4. **Targets:** ≥ 44 px everywhere — letter bar, chips, player controls, prev/next, card links. Grid *images* may be smaller than 44 px in neither dimension on phone (2-column layout guarantees this at 320 px).
5. **Alt text:** every exhibit image carries its catalog `altText`; purely decorative images are hidden from assistive tech; artwork containing essential lettering has that lettering transcribed in its alt/caption (several m-art pieces letter — the catalog pass owns this, Ada may sample-check).
6. **Dynamic announcements:** result counts and copy confirmations use one persistent polite live region per screen, updated once per deliberate change, in step with the visible results (§2.4/§3.2). Track changes announce title + voice in the player region. Focus is never moved by a filter, search, or show-more update; the activated control keeps focus and stays mounted.
7. **Motion:** no auto-advancing carousels anywhere in this design (by choice); any transition Mina adds honours `prefers-reduced-motion`; playback progress is not conveyed by motion alone (text time readout required).
8. **Reflow & resize:** works at 320 CSS px with no two-dimensional scrolling (hence the wrapping wing nav, §3.0), and at 200% text with no loss — including the player bar present, which is when vertical space gets tight.
9. **State never by colour alone:** current wing, selected voice toggle, playing track, applied filters — each carries a text/shape/position channel in addition to any colour (§5.1).
10. **Keyboard:** the entire primary flow (land → wing → piece → next piece → play a song → search) is operable by keyboard alone, including the progress slider; no drag-only or hover-only interactions exist in this design.
11. **FLAG for Ada:** the wrapped three-row phone wing nav (decided layout, §3.0 — full wing names, Dustin's ruling) vs. a "Galleries" disclosure at 320 px — her remaining item is confirmation that the three-row wrap satisfies the floor (gate AF-2); and the deep viewing ground's full pair set (§5.1).

## 6. Risks, open questions, and flags

### Risks (owned by the build/gate, listed so nobody meets them late)

- **R1 — SPA fallback swallowing a media URL.** The catastrophic failure mode: a live site's image or a shared song returns HTML. Mitigation is §2.3's ordered contract (media first, extensions are files, prefixes are files) plus a Quinn gate test set: a sample of real media URLs from every prefix, requested before and after the change, byte-compared in behaviour (status, content-type, range).
- **R2 — Catalog labour is the schedule.** The 218 dahlia titles are largely derivable from existing site data; the 85 m-art and the park/arcade pieces need human naming, grouping, and alt text. The museum cannot launch ahead of its catalog, and a half-named wing reads worse than a smaller complete one. Sequence: catalog pass commissioned against §4.3 in parallel with Mina's UI work, using a sample wing (Park) as the pilot.
- **R3 — CONFLICT FLAGGED: no-JS / prerender floor vs. pure SPA shell.** My playbook's house rule (and the SPA program's standing pattern, per Bea's validation on hues.flowers) is that public routes are prerendered with complete primary content, so a no-JavaScript visit and a search crawler see the real page, and local controls degrade to links with server-rendered state. The current technical ground truth (GOAL.md) describes SPA output under `_app/` with Worker fallback — a shell-first model. **House program standard wins over the simpler build:** my recommendation is prerender-then-hydrate for `/`, the five wing first-paints, and piece pages (the catalog is static data; these routes are prerenderable by construction), with in-place behaviour layered on after hydration exactly as §2.4 specifies. If Hue/the build choose shell-only, that is a recorded exception with a known cost (blank museum for no-JS visitors, weaker search presence for a public surface) — it should not happen silently. This is an engineering-shape decision for Hue, flagged here because it changes what "done" means for every screen above.
- **R4 — Performance on the Dahlias wing.** 218 thumbnails is a heavy page if built naively. The design's mitigations (transform-ladder thumbnails, lazy loading, 48-at-a-time reveal, reserved aspect boxes) are requirements, not optimisations; the build should carry a page-weight budget for `/dahlias` first paint and Quinn should measure it on a throttled phone profile.
- **R5 — Future seeds collision.** Handled structurally by R-6, but the day the `seeds/` prefix lands, someone must re-run the R1 test set including `seeds/` samples before the wing opens.

### Open questions for Dustin (3, each with my recommendation)

1. **Do all cultivar photos hang, or a curated subset?** My recommendation: **all hang** (put to Dustin as "all 412"; he decided all hang, and the ruling — which was about exclusion — stands). They are the collection — photographs of real Hues varieties, each with a name a visitor might search for — and the A–Z + search machinery makes the full set browsable rather than overwhelming. Curation effort goes to the *featured* band, not to excluding varieties. (Other wings stay curated subsets, per §2.1.) **Correction (gate F3/F14):** the "412" was a file count — 206 pool originals + 206 `opt/` copies; the wing holds **218 distinct varieties** (§2.1). And the rationale's original phrasing is corrected: the 206 pool images are game-pool imagery *of* the varieties, **not** "real photos" in the sense of the park's own photography — only the 35 round-2 varieties are park-photographed. The recommendation survives the honest framing (Dustin later kept two pool photos in the featured band knowingly); the description did not, and is fixed here.
2. **Songs: one exhibit per song, or one per file (38)?** The 38 files are 19 sung + 19 instrumental, and filenames show same-title pairs. My recommendation: **one exhibit per song (19), with the two versions as a switch on its track view** (`companionSlug`, §4.3) — a visitor thinks "Petals Open," then chooses sung or instrumental; the Listening Room list still shows all 38 rows for direct play, grouped under their song. If the catalog pass finds pairs that don't actually match, those stay separate exhibits — the data decides, pair by pair.
3. **Should the museum be findable (prerendered, indexable), or is shell-only SPA acceptable?** My recommendation: **prerender** (see R3). It's a public surface carrying Hues' name; a visitor arriving from search with a blank page, or a crawler indexing an empty shell, spends brand trust the park earned. The cost is build complexity, and it belongs to the hues.flowers SPA program's already-settled pattern rather than a new invention.
